# Customer study: would a street lend and borrow tools from a shared shelf

A fixture. It decides whether to run a pilot of a shared tool shelf in a street, and it does not decide a price. No respondent is real.

**Shape:** [customer study](../../customer-study.md)

## Question

- Do residents of one street say they would use a shared tool shelf, and do they act on it when asked to sign up?
- The decision: run a pilot in this street, or drop the idea.

## Sample

- Frame: the households of one fictional street, 40 in all, listed from the street's own notice board.
- Recruitment: a note through each door, then a visit to those who did not answer; no one is recruited through a friend of the team.
- Size: N = 12 for the interviews, because homogeneous groups reached saturation within 9 to 17 in the reviewed tests; stopped sooner by the rule below.

## Instrument

- A 20-minute interview from the guide below, recorded in notes only.
- A one-page sign-up sheet at the end, with the monthly price shown.

## Question order

1. When did you last need a tool you did not own?
2. What did you do about it?
3. What did that cost you, in money and in time?
4. How do you feel about lending a tool to a neighbour?
5. Only now: the shelf is described; would you use it?

## Incentive

- None. Nobody pays the respondents, and no one is promised a place in the pilot.
- The sign-up sheet carries a real cost: a deposit of a small sum, paid by the household, refunded if the pilot does not run.

## Analysis plan

- Each interview is coded for needs, with the codes listed before the first interview.
- The sign-up rate is reported beside the stated interest, as a ratio.

## Stopping rule

- Stop after three consecutive interviews that add no new code, or at N = 12.

## Limits

- One street, so the result says nothing of other streets.
- Neighbours may say yes to avoid awkwardness (social desirability).
- A deposit screens out the households with little spare cash.
