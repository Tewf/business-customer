# State of the art

Step 3 of the review. For each question, the best evidence, kept apart from the practice most adopted. Sources are in [review](review.md).

**Shape:** [state of the art](https://github.com/Tewf/reader-ergonomics-review/blob/c43f2770000a9af5c2aa3cbc6f997da7a7e13b34/shapes/state-of-the-art.md)

## Q1 How many interviews does a segment need?

| Record | Holder | What it says | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Best evidence | Hennink 2022 | 23 tests: saturation at 9 to 17 interviews for homogeneous samples with narrow aims | measured, descriptive |
| Upper bound | Guest 2020 | a stopping rule with a threshold, checked on three coded datasets | supported with caveat |
| Limit | Braun 2019 | saturation as a concept is questioned for thematic analysis (title only) | supported with caveat |
| Most adopted | Nielsen 2000 | five users find most problems, from L = 31% per user | measured, descriptive |

## Q2 How far do stated and revealed answers differ?

| Record | Holder | What it says | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Best evidence | Webb 2006 | d = 0.66 in intention gives d = 0.36 in behaviour, over 47 tests | measured |
| Upper bound | Ding 2005 | an incentive on the choice improved out-of-sample prediction of purchase | measured |
| Limit | Rakotonarivo 2016 | hypothetical bias was common in 107 choice studies | measured, descriptive |
| Most adopted | Blank 2013 | ask customers before building, and count what they say | convention |

## Q3 What does a choice study need in sample and design?

| Record | Holder | What it says | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Best evidence | de Bekker-Grob 2015 | a power calculation for the coefficients, step by step | convention |
| Upper bound | McFadden 2000 | a mixed logit can approximate any random utility model | measured |
| Limit | Rakotonarivo 2016 | results changed with small design changes in 45% of outcomes with a reliability measure | measured, descriptive |
| Most adopted | Bierlaire 2026 Biogeme | free packages estimate the models; the design is the analyst's | convention |

## Q4 What must a protocol fix before the first question?

| Record | Holder | What it says | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Best evidence | Nosek 2018 | the questions and the analysis plan, before the outcomes are seen | convention |
| Upper bound | Tong 2007 | a three-domain checklist for reporting an interview study | convention |
| Limit | none found | no protocol shape for a customer study | not found, in these searches |
| Most adopted | Fitzpatrick 2013 | a handbook of how to ask, with no protocol | convention |

## Q5 How do the errors of asking arise?

| Record | Holder | What it says | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Best evidence | Tourangeau 2007 | misreporting is common and situational | supported with caveat |
| Upper bound | Krosnick 1991 | a theory that names the shortcuts of a tired respondent | supported with caveat |
| Limit | Krosnick 1999 | part of what was blamed on social desirability has other causes | supported with caveat |
| Most adopted | Fitzpatrick 2013 | ask about past behaviour, not about the idea | convention |

## Q6 How is a group whose demand differs found?
