# Review: eight strands

Step 2 of the review, read on 2026-11-04. It groups the 40 sources by strand and answers Q1 to Q6 of [frame](frame.md). Surveys first in each strand.

**Shape:** [review](https://github.com/Tewf/reader-ergonomics-review/blob/c43f2770000a9af5c2aa3cbc6f997da7a7e13b34/shapes/review.md)

- A figure read in an abstract is marked (secondary); a work located only gives no figure.

## Strand 0: existing reviews

- 23 empirical tests of saturation: interviews reached it within 9 to 17, focus groups within 4 to 8, for homogeneous populations (Hennink 2022, abstract, secondary). Measured, descriptive.
- 107 articles on environmental choice experiments: results changed with small design changes in 45% of outcomes with a reliability measure, and hypothetical bias was common (Rakotonarivo 2016, abstract, secondary). Measured, descriptive.
- A macro-scale analysis of hypothetical bias in stated choice, and a paper on its sources and mitigation (Haghani 2021a, Haghani 2021b, located only). No figure taken.
- No review joining interviews, surveys, choice models and pre-orders was found (protocol, section 3). Not found, in these searches.

## Strand 1: choice models and their design (Q3)

- Demand is estimated from the choices people make, in the random utility model of McFadden 1974 (located only) and his lecture (McFadden 2001, located only). Convention.
- The textbook runs from logit to mixed logit, individual-level parameters and Bayesian procedures (Train 2009, contents page read). Convention.
- Any choice model derived from random utility can be approximated as closely as one pleases by a mixed logit (McFadden 2000, abstract, secondary). Measured, as a proof and an application.
- A choice experiment is not conjoint analysis; the two differ in what they model (Louviere 2010, title, located only). Supported with caveat.
- Sample size theory exists for stated choice data but not for the power of tests on coefficients; a step-by-step guide supplies it (de Bekker-Grob 2015, abstract, secondary). Convention.
- A guide to conducting experiments for decisions (Lancsar 2008) and three task-force reports on a checklist, on design and on analysis (Bridges 2011, Johnson 2013, Hauber 2016) were located only. Convention.
- The packages Biogeme and mlogit estimate random utility models free of charge (Bierlaire 2026 Biogeme, Croissant 2026 mlogit, pages read). Convention.
- A minimum sample for a good with no prior estimate was not found stated in a source read (protocol, strings F2, F8, F9). Not found, in these searches.

## Strand 2: the errors of asking (Q5)

- Misreporting on sensitive topics is common and largely situational: respondents edit what they say to avoid embarrassment (Tourangeau 2007, abstract, secondary). Supported with caveat.
- A review of the determinants of social desirability bias exists (Krumpal 2013, located only). Supported with caveat.
- When a full answer takes effort, some respondents give a satisfactory one: the first alternative, agreement, the status quo, no differentiation, "don't know" or a random pick (Krosnick 1991, abstract, secondary). Supported with caveat.
- Respondents read a question by the norms of everyday conversation, and some error once blamed on social desirability has other causes (Krosnick 1999, abstract, secondary). Supported with caveat.
- Question order effects are named in four titles found by string F11; they were screened, not read, and are not in the pool. Convention, by title only.

## Strand 3: stated and revealed answers, and experiments (Q2)

- Across 47 experimental tests, a change in intention of d = 0.66 led to a change in behaviour of d = 0.36 (Webb 2006, abstract, secondary). Measured.
- An incentive-aligned choice conjoint predicted out-of-sample purchases better than the hypothetical one, in a restaurant field experiment and a snack study (Ding 2005, abstract, secondary). Measured.
- The gap on price, 21% on average for consumer goods, is [held by business-pricing](https://github.com/Tewf/business-pricing/blob/0d746beca6c5f2395e434ddbbc38c9d5a73d2cb2/evidence/willingness-to-pay/review.md) and not repeated.
- A controlled experiment is the design that establishes a causal link from a change to user behaviour, say its practitioners (Kohavi 2009, abstract, secondary). Convention.
- In a randomized trial of 116 startups, those taught to test hypotheses like scientists performed better, pivoted more and did not drop out more (Camuffo 2020, abstract, secondary). Measured.
- The gap is measured as an effect size of intention on behaviour, or as a ratio of stated to paid; both need a behaviour observed after the answer. Convention.

## Strand 4: interviews, and the number needed (Q1)

- In 60 interviews, saturation came within the first 12, and the main themes were present by 6 (Guest 2006, abstract, secondary). Measured, descriptive.
- A stopping rule uses a base size, a run length and a new information threshold, checked by bootstrapping on three coded datasets (Guest 2020, abstract, secondary). Supported with caveat.
- The discovery of usability problems fits a Poisson process in 11 studies; early tests estimate the number left, and a medium example is worth 16 evaluations at best ratio four (Nielsen 1993, abstract, secondary). Measured, descriptive.
- With 31% found by one user, n users find a share 1 - (1 - 0.31)^n of the problems, so five find most (Nielsen 2000, article read). Measured, descriptive.
- So an estimator of the unseen exists for problems and a stopping rule for themes; both are shown for narrow, homogeneous samples (Hennink 2022).
- A species-richness estimator for themes was not searched (protocol, string F33, not run). Not found, in these searches.

## Strand 5: protocols and reporting (Q4)

- A reporting checklist for interviews and focus groups came from 76 items of 22 checklists, in three domains: team, design, analysis (Tong 2007, abstract, secondary). Convention.
- A plan fixed before the outcomes are seen separates prediction from postdiction, and hindsight bias makes the plan necessary (Nosek 2018, abstract, secondary). Convention.
- The task-force reports ask a design and an analysis plan before a choice study (Bridges 2011, Johnson 2013, Hauber 2016, located only). Convention.
- A protocol shape for a customer study, with its sample, question order and incentive, was not found (protocol, strings F26 to F28). Not found, in these searches.

## Strand 6: segments and heterogeneity (Q6)

- Ignoring that consumers differ in preferences or in how they choose misreads market structure and segments, shown in simulations (Kamakura 1996, abstract, secondary). Measured, as a simulation.
- A mixed logit lets coefficients vary across people (McFadden 2000, abstract, secondary). Measured, as a proof.
- An open-access book walks through each decision of a segmentation analysis (Dolnicar 2018, publisher blurb). Convention.

## Strand 7: practice

- Customer discovery means testing a need with customers before building, and the lean start-up method puts a test of a hypothesis ahead of a plan (Blank 2013, summary and opening read). Convention.
- Questions about past behaviour and not about the idea are the handbook's rule (Fitzpatrick 2013, page read, example only). Convention.
- A founder recruits users by hand at the start (Graham 2013, essay read, example only). Convention.
- A weekly interview and a snapshot of each one are one practice (Torres 2024, page read, example only). Convention.
- The method claims validated learning from a minimum product (Ries 2026 Lean, page read, example only). Convention.
- The only randomized test of a taught method found is Camuffo 2020; no test of an unstructured interview round was found. Not found, in these searches.
